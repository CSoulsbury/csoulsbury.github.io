Life History Research

How organisms grow, breed, age and die, and why it differs between them

Welcome to the research home page of Prof. Carl Soulsbury. My group studies the evolution of life history strategies in animals and plants, with field sites in Britain and abroad, and how we can measure and improve the welfare of wild animals. If you are interested in joining, get in contact.

  • Black grouse Lyrurus tetrix
  • Red fox Vulpes vulpes
  • Katydids & crickets
  • Insect sperm & eggs
  • Plant–pollinator interactions
  • Wild animal welfare

Latest news

  1. September 2026

    Congratulations to Ruben Culqui Salazar, who passed his PhD viva on the distribution and ecology of caecilians in Ecuador. Well done Dr Culqui Salazar!

  2. August 2026

    Congratulations to Hafiza Maira Munir on passing her PhD viva, for her work on lesser wax moths. Well done Dr Munir!

  3. June 2026

    Graziella Iossa and I have been awarded a Leverhulme Trust grant to study how insect eggs and sperm coevolve, building on our HFSP work on sperm evolution. The project funds a three-year PhD studentship and a three-year postdoc. Read more on Graziella's site.

  4. June 2026

    Delighted to say we have been awarded a Wild Animal Initiative Challenge Grant to develop, test and validate indicators of wild insect welfare. Using Dubia cockroaches, two-spotted crickets and carrion beetles, we'll measure stress responses, fat reserves and movement, check them against cognitive bias tests, and then take the most practical indicators out to wild carrion beetle populations.

  5. May 2026

    Congratulations to Rebecca Chen, who passed her PhD viva on the genomics of sexually selected traits in black grouse, co-supervised with Joe Hoffman and Kees van Oers. Well done Dr Chen!

Many thanks to my hosts Inge Mueller, Wolfgang Fiedler and Daniel Zuniga; it was a great visit. At the end of the month I also visited Bielefeld University to present our HFSP work on sperm evolution. Great to catch up with Joe Hoffman, Rebecca Chen and Kees van Oers.
