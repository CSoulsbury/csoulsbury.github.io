Welcome to the research home page of Prof. Carl Soulsbury. My group studies the evolution of life history strategies in animals and plants, with field sites in Britain and abroad. If you are interested in joining, get in contact.

Latest news

  1. October 2024

    In October I was invited to the Max Planck Institute of Animal Behaviour to talk about wild animal welfare. Many thanks to my hosts Inge Mueller, Wolfgang Fiedler and Daniel Zuniga; it was a great visit. At the end of the month I also visited Bielefeld University to present our HFSP work on sperm evolution. Great to catch up with Joe Hoffman, Rebecca Chen and Kees van Oers.

  2. August 2024

    Busy summer so far. Blake Morton, Simon Clegg and I have been awarded a Wild Animal Initiative grant to study parasites and pathogens in urban wildlife, with a new PhD starting in October. We've also had several papers published, including fox genetics in Australia and sperm–egg coevolution.

  3. January 2024

    Some personal news (which I never really like sharing): I was promoted to Professor of Animal Ecology.

  4. September 2023

    Our HFSP team took part in the Biology of Sperm conference in Sweden. A fabulous time hearing about sperm biology in a wonderful setting. Already looking forward to 2025.

  5. March 2023

    We had the pleasure of visiting Prof. Lisa Fauci, Prof. Ricardo Cortez and Dr Adnan Morshed at Tulane University, New Orleans, as part of our HFSP project.

  6. November 2022

    In spring we were awarded an HFSP grant to study sperm cell evolution. Find out more. Several papers have been accepted, including one on ear pinnae in katydids and how they let katydids hear bats, a study on erythrocyte size in reptiles and tuatara that began as a summer project, and Liberty Gray's first paper on UV stress and floral traits. Well done all.

  7. October 2021

    Birds are amazing athletes adapted to their lifestyle. In a new paper in Integrative and Comparative Biology we show how energetic lifestyle is reflected in avian erythrocyte size and shape. Congratulations to Jess Dobson on her first paper and on submitting her MSc by Research thesis.

  8. October 2021

    New commentary paper out on the need for better welfare reporting in wild animal studies. It grew out of a Twitter discussion about a techniques paper that left out basic welfare reporting needed to compare methods. The BES journals have since rethought how they handle animal ethics, which I think is an excellent outcome. Read the commentary.

  9. August 2021

    Welcome to Rebecca Chen, a new PhD student working with Joe Hoffman, Kees van Oers and me on black grouse genetics.
