Lab members

Prof. Carl Soulsbury

Professor of Animal Ecology · Group leader

My main interest is the evolution of life history strategies. My work focuses mainly on the behavioural and evolutionary ecology of vertebrates, but has expanded to include plant and insect life histories.

I did my undergraduate degree at the University of Aberdeen, where I was awarded the Nicol Prize for the top zoology graduate. I then did a PhD on red fox Vulpes vulpes dispersal at the University of Bristol, supervised by Prof. Stephen Harris and Dr Phil Baker, followed by a postdoc on black grouse behavioural ecology at the University of Jyväskylä. I am now Professor of Animal Ecology at the University of Lincoln.

Former lab members (14)

Rebecca Chen

PhD, viva May 2026 · Hoffman lab, Bielefeld

Rebecca's PhD used state-of-the-art sequencing to unravel the genetic architecture of sexually selected traits in the lekking black grouse Lyrurus tetrix, including inbreeding depression and the role of DNA methylation. Co-supervised with Joe Hoffman and Kees van Oers.

Libbi Gray

MSc by Research

Libbi studied floral phenotypes and how they may be affected by changes in UV-B radiation, using herbarium specimens and greenhouse and growth-chamber experiments. She went on to a PhD at the University of Edinburgh.

Jess Dobson

MSc by Research

Jess studied the quality of sexual signals in male black grouse: carotenoid pigments in the red eye combs (HPLC and microtomy), and how feather structure influences colour. She is now doing a PhD with Prof. Matthew Shawkey at Ghent University.

Dr Matti Kervinen

PhD, 2010–2014

Matti studied the ecological factors affecting lifetime fitness of black grouse males, separating what contributes most to variation in reproductive success and survival at different ages. He now works for the Finnish Wildlife Agency (Riistakeskus).

Dr Ben Chivers

PhD

Ben completed a PhD with Prof. Fernando Montealegre-Z, using µ-scanning laser Doppler vibrometry in a large comparative study of stridulation and wing mechanics across katydid species.

Sarah Harris

MSc by Research, 2016

Sarah studied the red eye combs of black grouse and how they relate to female mate choice, heritability and individual scheduling of growth. She went on to work as a research assistant on the PEARL project.

Christian Pulver

Postgraduate

Christian measured the mechanical contribution of the multiple acoustic ports to a single katydid ear, using three laser systems and several species with different ear morphology.

Dan Veitch

MSc by Research
